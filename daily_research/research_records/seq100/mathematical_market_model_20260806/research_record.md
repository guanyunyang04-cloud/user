# Mathematical Market-Model Research Record

Status: retrospective research, no model selected, 2026-08-06; post-run
mean/scale audit added after the final forecast files were generated

Audience: maintainers of Daily Research and the future stock-level model
implementation. This record is an internal developer/research guide, not a
claim of a deployable strategy.

## Decision Enabled

This record decides whether the corrected data justify a privileged market
model class and what must be learned before an executable portfolio model can
be specified. The answer is deliberately limited: no candidate is promoted to
production, no feature family is frozen, and no profitability claim is made.

## Evidence Contract

The experiment reads the certified `compact_core` input through
`seq100_mathematical_market_model.py`.

- Formal dates: 2012 through 2025; 2010-2011 are burn-in only.
- Complete corrected input: 4,191,476 candidate rows and 557 fields.
- Market observation: 3,400 trading dates, because the 54 `market_*` fields are
  date-level values repeated across candidates.
- Input fingerprint:
  `a2bea9ed174b0a04ea2e97af475e9b4f1da79d739288a0cd92114998e31784ae`.
- Forbidden year: 2026. The loader rejects a different fingerprint, row count,
  training flag, date count, or a 2026 observation.
- Development folds: 2017-2022. Retrospective outer folds: 2023-2025.
  The outer years have already been inspected in this project and are not an
  untouched confirmation holdout.

The diagnostic target is the next market equal-weight log return,

`g[t+1] = log(1 + market_all__ret1_mean[t+1])`,

with cumulative horizon target

`G[t,h] = sum(j=1..h) g[t+j]`, for `h` in `{1,2,5,10,20}`.

This target is a market-state diagnostic. It is not a stock return, an entry
fill, an exit fill, or an executable account objective.

## First Principles

### Information, prediction, and action are different objects

Let `F_t` be the sigma-field generated by all point-in-time information
available at the signal close. For an executable stock path define a random
vector `Z_t` containing next-open entry, legal T+1 sale outcomes, intermediate
path extrema, costs, and execution-state censoring. The forecasting object is

`Q_t(dz) = P(Z_t in dz | F_t)`.

A portfolio rule is a separate map from a forecast and a stated utility to an
action. For long-only weights with cash, a mathematically explicit later
decision problem is, for example,

`max_w E[ log(1 + w' R_net) ] - lambda * CVaR_alpha(-w' R_net)`

subject to `w_i >= 0`, `sum(w_i) <= 1`, turnover and capacity constraints, and
`||w||_0 <= K`. The cash inequality is essential: if the lower confidence
bound of net utility is not positive, zero risky weight is an admissible and
often optimal action.

For a one-position fraction `f`, the local expansion

`E log(1 + f R_net) ~= f*mu_net - 0.5*f^2*variance_net`

shows why a fixed event such as “make 8%” cannot be a fundamental target. Costs
shift `mu_net`; tail risk and position size enter through the variance and the
non-quadratic remainder. The model must preserve the distribution of the
execution-relevant path, not just a threshold label.

No-arbitrage results constrain discounted prices under a chosen pricing measure;
they do not specify the physical conditional mean of an A-share return or
guarantee a positive forecasting edge. A profit objective therefore requires an
economic utility and an execution contract supplied by the project, not a
formula selected by mathematics alone.

### The Markov kernel is a hypothesis, not a theorem about this data

If there were a sufficiently informative state `S_t` such that

`P(S[t+1], R[t+1] | F_t) = K(S[t+1], R[t+1] | S_t)`,

and if the kernel were sufficiently time-homogeneous, then the
Chapman-Kolmogorov equation would produce a coherent multi-day law by
composition. This gives the iterated-kernel class a useful coherence property.
It does not prove that the current 557 fields, or a PCA compression of them,
are sufficient. It also does not remove long memory, structural breaks,
suspensions, price limits, T+1 constraints, or accumulated approximation error.

The experiment therefore keeps three distinct hypotheses alive:

1. an iterated one-step transition kernel;
2. direct conditional laws for each horizon;
3. a joint law for a multi-horizon path vector.

An augmented or long-memory volatility state is a fourth challenger. A kernel
can be promoted only if its time-out-of-sample scores, calibration, and path
dependence are competitive with the alternatives. Coherence is a benefit, not
evidence of accuracy.

## Feature Construction From Mathematics

### Causal minute statistics

For a valid 5-minute bar return `r_{i,t,j}`,

`RV_{i,t} = sqrt(sum_j r_{i,t,j}^2)`

is a quadratic-variation proxy. Downside and upside semivolatility are

`RV^- = sqrt(sum_j r_{i,t,j}^2 1{r_{i,t,j}<0})`,
`RV^+ = sqrt(sum_j r_{i,t,j}^2 1{r_{i,t,j}>0})`.

The implementation also uses the within-day open-close return and normalized
amount entropy

`H_t = -sum_j p_j log(p_j) / log(48)`, `p_j = amount_j / sum_k amount_k`.

These are computed only from bars available by the signal close and then
cross-sectionally averaged. The averages are market-state proxies; averaging
individual quadratic variations omits cross-stock covariance and is not being
called the realized variance of the equal-weight portfolio.

### Why trailing volatility state is a candidate

The heterogeneous autoregressive (HAR) construction uses causal trailing means
of `log(RV)` over 1, 5, 22, and 66 dates. The windows represent daily, weekly,
monthly, and quarterly aggregation scales; they are a testable long-memory
parameterization, not a claim that these calendar lengths are unique.
Downside/upside semivariance balance is included as an asymmetry coordinate.

The stock-level feature contract remains open. A later stock model should group
features by information timing and economic role, remove constants and
semantic duplicates, and first test incremental conditional information after
removing common market and industry factors. A large row count does not turn
date-clustered market observations into independent samples.

## Candidate Models and Learning Rules

| Class | Learned object | Main assumption under test |
|---|---|---|
| Static Gaussian / Student-t | unconditional law of each `G[t,h]` | no conditional state dependence beyond a horizon prior |
| EWMA Student-t | time-varying conditional variance with half-lives 5, 20, 60 | volatility clustering is useful even without covariates |
| GARCH Gaussian / Student-t | parametric conditional variance recursion | short-memory volatility recursion is adequate |
| Direct ridge Student-t | horizon-specific conditional location and optional log scale | direct horizon law can beat iterated accumulation |
| Direct HAR Student-t | conditional law using the causal long-memory state | volatility memory survives the 54-field state |
| Joint direct Gaussian / Student-t | one covariance law for `(G_1,G_2,G_5,G_10,G_20)` | horizon dependence is learned jointly |
| Iterated PCA-VAR Gaussian | low-rank one-step state transition and its coherent path covariance | a compressed approximately Markov state is sufficient |
| Augmented iterated PCA-VAR | the same kernel with HAR volatility coordinates | state augmentation repairs residual memory |

Direct ridge models use three declared penalties. After robust standardization,
`alpha = n * penalty` corresponds to mean squared error plus
`penalty * ||beta||^2`, so the penalty has comparable meaning as the expanding
training sample grows. The grid is a sensitivity family, not an implicit choice
of a winning regularizer.

Student-t predictive scales are handled consistently: when a forecast is
specified by a conditional standard deviation `s` and degrees of freedom `nu`,
the scipy scale is `s*sqrt((nu-2)/nu)`. This matters for EWMA, GARCH, and the
joint Student-t models. An earlier prototype mixed variance and Student-t scale
parameters; its output is not used as evidence here.

The direct heteroskedastic prototype fits a conditional mean, models log
residual square, and calibrates the resulting scale. It is a transparent
benchmark, not a final stochastic-volatility estimator. Negative skewness and
jumps remain an explicit unresolved challenger: symmetric Student-t laws are
useful baselines but cannot be assumed to be the final tail family.

## Proper Scores and Inference

For a univariate predictive law `F` and observation `y`, the primary log score
is `log f(y)`. CRPS is

`CRPS(F,y) = integral (F(u) - 1{y <= u})^2 du`.

Higher log score and lower CRPS are better. PIT values, central 50% and 90%
coverage, and RMSE are calibration diagnostics; RMSE alone is not a proper
distributional objective. For the joint path, the experiment reports
multivariate log score, energy score, and variogram score. The variogram score
is included because an energy score can look acceptable while dependence
between horizons is wrong.

All scores are calculated per date. For a model comparison define

`d_t = score_model,t - score_baseline,t`

for log score, and reverse the sign for losses such as CRPS. The comparison
uses Newey-West/Bartlett HAC bandwidths 20 and 60, circular moving-block
bootstrap lengths 20 and 60 with 2,000 repetitions, and a White max-statistic
Reality Check over the declared family. These procedures quantify serial
dependence and data snooping; they do not restore an untouched holdout after
the outer years have been inspected.

## Empirical Results

### Market state diagnostics

| Quantity | Corrected-data result | Mathematical implication |
|---|---:|---|
| daily equal-weight log-return skew | `-1.048` | symmetric laws are only baselines |
| excess kurtosis | `5.469` | Gaussian tails are inadequate as a sole law |
| absolute-return ACF(1) | `0.252` | conditional scale is persistent |
| log mean minute RV ACF(1/5/20/60/120) | `0.835/0.699/0.567/0.381/0.296` | volatility memory is much longer than one day |
| GPH `d` for log mean RV | `0.640` (SE `0.085`) | long-memory diagnostic supports an augmented-state challenger |
| robust PCA PC1 / first 3 | `28.4%` / `65.2%` | the old “PC1 about 90%” statement is not reproduced |
| ordinary PCA PC1 / first 3 | `26.9%` / `56.4%` | collinearity exists, but not one-dimensional sufficiency |
| return-only 12-field PCA PC1 / first 3 | `53.1%` / `89.5%` | return subspace is more concentrated than the full state |

HAR, AR(1), and constant forecasts of next-date log mean RV were evaluated on
2017-2025 folds. HAR has lower mean RMSE (`0.1097` versus `0.1158` for AR(1)
and `0.2116` for constant) and higher mean Gaussian log score (`0.7263` versus
`0.6580` and `0.0432`). HAR is not perfect: its test residual Ljung-Box signal
remains significant in several folds (notably 2018-2020 and 2023-2024), while
other years are compatible with white residuals. The result rejects the claim
that a one-day memory state is already empirically sufficient, while not
proving a particular fractional model.

### Marginal market-return comparison

The following are pooled over the 707 outer dates. “Gain” is the unadjusted
mean log-score improvement over the static Student-t baseline; the winner was
chosen only for descriptive reporting after the family was run.

| Horizon | Best log-score candidate | Gain | Best CRPS candidate | White RC p (block 20 / 60) |
|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | EWMA Student-t, half-life 5 | `+0.0656` | direct HAR Student-t | `0.032 / 0.026` |
| 2 | direct HAR Student-t, ridge `1e-3` | `+0.0765` | direct HAR Student-t | `0.047 / 0.084` |
| 5 | EWMA Student-t, half-life 5 | `+0.0784` | direct HAR Student-t | `0.143 / 0.135` |
| 10 | EWMA Student-t, half-life 20 | `+0.0808` | direct HAR Student-t | `0.219 / 0.244` |
| 20 | EWMA Student-t, half-life 20 | `+0.0616` | direct HAR Student-t | `0.503 / 0.545` |

The short-horizon pooled gains are encouraging diagnostic evidence, but the
winner changes with horizon and score. The low Reality Check p-values at H=1
and H=2 are not confirmatory evidence: 2023-2025 were reused for research
choices, and the declared family contains many correlated variants. The full
HAC, bootstrap, PIT, coverage, and per-date records are in
`daily_research/output/path_policy/studies/seq100_mathematical_market_model/`.

Feature observations are similarly conditional, not causal attribution:

- At the fixed `1e-2` ridge penalty, HAR direct models beat market-only direct
  models by `0.0276/0.0388/0.0498/0.0708/0.1167` log-score points at
  H=1/2/5/10/20. The range across all declared penalties reaches `0.1754` at
  H=20, but EWMA still often scores higher against the static baseline.
- Concatenating all 54 market fields with the five current minute fields has a
  small, unstable pooled difference from market-only at the fixed `1e-2`
  penalty: `+0.0137/+0.0108/+0.0024/+0.0035/-0.0102` at H=1/2/5/10/20.
  H=1-2 remain positive across all three penalties, H=5-10 change sign, and
  H=20 is non-positive. Annual signs also change. The earlier sentence saying
  that concatenation was worse at H=1-10 for every penalty was based on a
  pre-final summary and is retired; the final per-date forecast file controls.
- Minute-only models also score above the 54-field market-only direct model in
  the pooled comparison. This can reflect lower estimation variance as well as
  information in the minute coordinates, so it is not an incremental-factor
  or causal claim.
- The three ridge penalties produce close but non-identical results. No
  penalty is selected.

### Conditional mean versus conditional scale

A post-run audit of the final `marginal_forecasts.parquet` separates return
location accuracy from distributional accuracy. The comparison uses the same
707 outer dates, the expanding static Student-t location as the squared-error
baseline, and the full declared forecast family.

The best descriptive location forecast has pooled OOS R-squared of
`0.0201/0.0303/0.0293/0.0370/0.0562` at H=1/2/5/10/20. These positive values
are preliminary magnitude evidence, not a selected alpha. The corresponding
family-level White Reality Check p-values for squared-error improvement are
`0.342/0.263/0.417/0.532/0.499` with 20-date blocks and
`0.311/0.296/0.424/0.568/0.502` with 60-date blocks. None supports a
selection-adjusted conditional-mean claim. Directional hit-rate gains are also
not family-level significant; several high-R-squared forecasts have lower raw
sign accuracy than the positive-mean static baseline.

The homoskedastic and heteroskedastic direct-market variants at a given ridge
penalty have exactly the same forecast location and Student-t degrees of
freedom. Their log-score difference therefore isolates the fitted conditional
scale. At penalty `1e-2`, that scale contribution is
`+0.0380/+0.0509/+0.0454/+0.0185/-0.0202` nats per date at
H=1/2/5/10/20. A four-corner Shapley decomposition against the static
Student-t baseline assigns a negative location contribution to this 54-field
direct model at every horizon; its short-horizon gain is carried by scale.

Applying the same location-versus-distribution Shapley decomposition to the
reported horizon winner assigns about `88%-94%` of its pooled log-score gain
to scale and tail-distribution parameters, not location. This decomposition is
descriptive because the fitted law is imperfect and the outer dates have been
inspected, but it materially sharpens the conclusion: the strongest evidence
found so far concerns conditional risk, not return direction.

### Joint path comparison

The best joint direct HAR Student-t model improves joint log score by only
`0.0103` over the joint static Student-t baseline. Its energy-score improvement
is about `0.00117`; the best variogram-score improvement is about `0.00091`
from a direct market Student-t variant. White Reality Check p-values are
approximately `0.83/0.78` for joint log score, `0.41/0.44` for energy score,
and `0.53/0.55` for variogram score at block lengths 20/60. No joint model is
supported as a superior path law.

The iterated kernels are coherent and positive-definite, but remain below the
Student-t volatility and direct HAR candidates on marginal scores. This is
evidence against privileging iteration, not evidence against ever using a
kernel.

## Conclusions and Boundaries

1. The corrected data support a distributional, time-varying volatility
   requirement for the market diagnostic. This is an empirical hypothesis
   worth carrying into stock-level work, not a selected production model.
2. Long-memory volatility is visible and partially forecastable. HAR reduces
   but does not eliminate residual dependence, so the Markov sufficiency claim
   remains unverified.
3. The 54-field state is collinear but not one-dimensional. The historical PC1
   and forced-single-kernel claims are retired.
4. Direct, iterated, and joint classes remain genuinely different. No class
   wins every horizon, score, and dependence diagnostic.
5. Positive descriptive OOS R-squared values do not survive family-level
   selection correction, directional accuracy is weak, and roughly `88%-94%`
   of the reported winning log-score gain is distributional. The current
   evidence is therefore a risk-forecast result, not directional alpha.
6. Nothing in this experiment estimates executable stock returns, net costs,
   fill probabilities, portfolio covariance, or account wealth. It cannot
   justify a trading rule or a profit claim.

## Required Next Research

The next implementation should be a new stock-level contract, not more tuning
of this market-only surface:

1. Freeze `F_t`, next-open entry, T+1-compatible sale windows, finite horizon,
   costs, capacity, and explicit right-censoring/execution-state labels.
2. Decompose stock returns into market/industry factors and an idiosyncratic
   conditional law; score both common-risk and residual forecasts.
3. Compare direct, joint, iterated, HAR/rough-volatility, and skew/jump-tail
   candidates inside nested chronological folds. Feature and penalty selection
   must occur inside the inner folds, not in 2023-2025.
4. Set the forecast gate before observing winners: a selection-adjusted lower
   confidence bound on score gain must exceed a cost-derived minimum relevant
   effect, with simultaneous regime tolerances.
5. Only after a forecast gate passes, solve the frozen cash-permitted
   log-growth/CVaR decision problem and reserve an unused confirmation period.

An 8% event can remain a descriptive report slice. It is not a theorem,
training oracle, or portfolio objective.
